# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Starling Rise primarily serves English learners aged 6–11 using shared or family
phones and tablets, with reliable internet access. Learners may be early readers
in English, so they need clear English paired with Traditional Chinese, simple
choices, and forgiving interactions. Teachers and administrators provision the
accounts and learning work through the existing web application; the native app
is focused on the learner's daily reading, speaking, spelling, feedback, and
progress workflows.

## Product Purpose

Starling Rise gives young learners a direct, native way to complete assigned
English practice without navigating a browser. It should make the next useful
activity obvious, preserve a learner's identity across classes and terms, and
help each learner hear and see their progress. Success means a learner can sign
in with minimal adult help, complete work confidently, recover from ordinary
mistakes or connectivity failures, and understand what to do next.

## Brand Personality

Warm, optimistic, and energetic. The Starling Rise identity connects flight with
student progress and uses the promise “Every voice can rise.” The interface
should encourage effort and growth without feeling childish, noisy, or
competitive.

## Anti-references

There are no named product references to imitate. Avoid dense administrative
interfaces, overstimulating game dashboards, casino-like rewards, cartoon clutter,
decorative motion, and visual complexity that competes with the learning task.
Do not make the learner experience feel like a repackaged website.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next learning action unmistakable.
2. Encourage progress without pressure or comparison.
3. Design for independent use by an early reader, while keeping recovery paths
   clear for an assisting adult.
4. Keep English and Traditional Chinese guidance consistent and concise.
5. Use familiar native interaction patterns and preserve learner work through
   recoverable failures.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support VoiceOver and TalkBack with meaningful labels, logical focus order, and
announced state changes. Use at least 44-point iOS and 48-dp Android touch targets,
readable contrast, guarded dynamic type, and reduced-motion behavior. Do not rely
on color, sound, English fluency, or precise motor control alone to communicate
meaning. Keep child-facing language positive and avoid labels that frame a learner
as failing or deficient.
